01 / The North Star

Not just a better graduate school.
A better way to work together.

Imagine a university where every student interaction feels supported, every office knows how it contributes, and good ideas move easily from one part of campus to another.

“Pieces together to raise everyone up.”

The opportunity is to make Graduate Studies a connector: linking academic programs, continuing education, faculty expertise, research support, technology, and community partners around meaningful outcomes.

The friction to address

Today, too much depends on navigating complexity.

  • Prospective students must piece together the path from curiosity to enrollment.
  • Faculty and staff often recreate resources or solve similar problems separately.
  • Unclear handoffs can slow admissions, support, and research processes.
  • Professional learning opportunities are not always connected across campus.
The future to create

Tomorrow, connections become the advantage.

  • Students experience one understandable, supportive journey.
  • Faculty, staff, and students collaborate on real institutional challenges.
  • Clear process ownership supports confident, empowered teams.
  • University expertise becomes accessible learning for campus and community.

Connect expertise to opportunity

04 / Turn the idea into action

Start small. Make it visible.
Measure what changes.

A one-semester demonstration could prove the model before asking the university to scale it.

PILOT 01

Two faculty design partnerships

IDLT student teams work with volunteer instructor SMEs to improve a real Canvas module, assessment, or student learning experience.

PILOT 02

One university PD module

Design a reusable, accessible professional learning module using common outcomes, format, and evaluation criteria.

PILOT 03

One practical AI workflow guide

Identify a frequent faculty or staff task, test an appropriate AI-supported approach, and publish an approved step-by-step guide.

A common project standard

  • Clear audience and documented learning need
  • Measurable learning outcomes
  • Co-design with faculty or staff subject-matter experts
  • Accessible learning experience and quality review
  • Feedback, impact measures, and implementation guide

What success looks like

  • Graduate students demonstrate work for professional portfolios
  • Faculty and staff report that resources meet real needs
  • Projects show quality, usability, and accessibility
  • Departments can reuse or adapt deliverables
  • Leaders have evidence to improve, scale, or stop
05 / The path forward

A 12-month roadmap to momentum

This approach combines near-term student experience improvements with longer-term collaboration and operating changes.

DAYS 1–30

Listen + map

Validate enrollment and process baselines, identify student barriers, meet partners, and define project eligibility.

DAYS 31–60

Choose + design

Approve three flagship workstreams, name owners, establish project templates, and select first pilots.

DAYS 61–90

Prototype + learn

Test student-facing improvements, begin learning design pilots, and review early feedback and risks.

MONTHS 4–12

Evaluate + grow

Expand successful pilots, document processes, evaluate CE opportunities, and report impact.

Don't measure innovation by how many new initiatives we announce. Measure it by how much easier we make meaningful work and learning.
06 / An invitation to collaborate

What we need now is the opportunity to test it.

A proposed provost-level invitation

Support a focused exploration of this connected model—not an immediate new office, a large financial commitment, or a university-wide rollout.

  • Convene a small cross-unit planning group with relevant leaders.
  • Authorize discovery and limited pilot scoping with participating programs and committees.
  • Identify an executive sponsor, project leads, and decision points.
  • Review initial evidence after one semester before considering expansion.

Andrea's leadership promise

Bring the right people together. Make the student experience simpler. Help faculty and staff contribute their best work. Create space for useful ideas—and follow through.
